Fiè allo Sciliar: finding the mountains again, on my wheels

  • 30 August to 4 September 2026
  • 6 days · 5 nights
  • Fiè allo Sciliar, South Tyrol

    This holiday meant something special to me: going back to the mountains, this time on my wheels, but with the same old wish to discover new places, enjoy the views and share lovely days with friends.

    We were a group, and we chose an approach we loved: some experiences we shared all together, while for others everyone picked their own route, according to their abilities and interests. Whenever possible, we all met up for lunch at the mountain hut.

    A perfect solution: everyone could enjoy the mountains in their own way, and then we all came together again at the table.

    Day 1 Sunday 30 AugustArriving in Fiè

    We set off at around 7.30, heading for Fiè allo Sciliar. We arrived at lunchtime and went straight to a first stop I had chosen with care.

    Lunch at the Pitschlmann

    The Pitschlmann is a traditional farmhouse I had picked for its wonderful panoramic terrace, its typical atmosphere and its excellent reviews. The kitchen serves South Tyrolean specialities and seasonal dishes, made with regional ingredients and produce from the farm.

    Accessibility · Toilet

    Accessibility at the Pitschlmann: the farmhouse also has an accessible toilet, an important detail for me.

    The perfect way to start our holiday: good food, a view and the mountains.

    Hotel Emmy

    After lunch we reached the Hotel Emmy, where you can drop off your luggage from 3 pm.

    Accessibility · Hotel

    Accessibility at the Hotel Emmy: I chose an accessible hotel, with an adapted bathroom featuring an accessible WC, grab rail and level-access shower with a shower chair.

    And then there’s the location: simply stunning, overlooking the village of Fiè, with breathtaking sunsets. Just enough time to unpack and we were ready for our first walk.

    The first walk: Hotel Emmy – Sant’Antonio – Hotel Emmy

    It’s a walk recommended by the hotel itself, starting and finishing right at the Hotel Emmy. The route passes through Sant’Antonio, among woods, meadows and wonderful views of the Sciliar (Schlern) massif.

    Accessibility · Route

    Accessibility of the walk: I did this route on my own wheels. Along the way there is a short climb where help from a companion is needed.

    A pleasant first walk, perfect for starting to discover the area and getting that wonderful feeling of being back in the mountains straight away.

    And to round off the day…

    Back at the hotel, a welcome aperitif on the Hotel Emmy terrace was waiting for us, on the house. Before dinner, another couple of friends who had set off later joined us: at last we were all together. After the toast, we enjoyed the excellent dinner included in our half board.

    A day that started early and was full of lovely moments ended in the best possible way: at the table, with the village of Fiè and the mountains in front of us, happy to be back in the mountains at last.

    Day 2 Monday 31 August · Fiè allo SciliarFrom Lake Fiè to Malga Tuff

    That day we all set off together, although we reached Lake Fiè in different ways: our friends walked there straight from the hotel, while I drove to the lake.

    We all met up at Lake Fiè, ready to start our day in the mountains.

    Getting to Lake Fiè

    Accessibility · Parking

    To get there by car I drove to the Lake Fiè car park. One important thing to know: you don’t need to go into the paid car park. Outside it there are clearly marked spaces reserved for disabled people.

    Once the car was parked, a short walk took me to the lake.

    Accessibility · Toilet

    There is also an accessible toilet at the car park.

    Lake Fiè

    Accessibility · Lake

    Lake Fiè is one of South Tyrol’s swimming lakes and, something especially important for me, it is accessible to disabled people too.

    A special chair is available that lets you go straight down into the water.

    A thoughtful touch that makes this lovely corner of the mountains even more inclusive: not just a place to admire, but a lake you can really experience and enjoy.

    From Lake Fiè to Malga Tuff

    Once we were all together, we started by going round the lake, then carried on towards the upper lake and on to Malga Tuff, an alpine farm.

    Accessibility · Route

    For this stretch we chose the wider path, avoiding the shortcut through the woods.

    The route

    Accessible on my wheels, but with some difficulty on certain stretches.

    When the slope got steeper, the surface of small loose stones made the Triride’s wheels lose grip. In those spots the motor couldn’t keep enough traction and I needed a push from my companion.

    Malga Tuff

    At last we reached Malga Tuff, surrounded by greenery and mountains.

    Here we found a small petting area with friendly little goats, while in the surrounding meadows we came across alpacas, horses, donkeys and even camels.

    A really unusual setting, which made the stop even more enjoyable. After the walk it was time to stop and enjoy the farm, the view and the whole mountain atmosphere.

    Accessibility · Toilet

    There’s an accessible toilet here too.

    Accessibility · Route

    The route · Coming back downhill

    The way down from Malga Tuff, on the other hand, was no problem at all: with the Triride set to cruise control, the wheels gripped the ground well and I managed it without any difficulty.

    So, with someone who can help on the harder uphill stretches, the route is doable; the way down turned out to be much easier.

    The upper lake

    On the way back we stopped to admire the upper lake once more, taking our time to enjoy this corner of nature before getting back in the car.

    Castel Presule and an aperitif

    But the day wasn’t over yet!

    We decided to stretch it out with a stop, all together by car, at Castel Presule (Schloss Prösels), the striking castle that overlooks the hamlet of Presule, in the municipality of Fiè allo Sciliar.

    It dates back to the Middle Ages and in the 16th century it was enlarged and turned into an elegant fortified residence. Today it is one of the area’s landmarks and blends beautifully into the South Tyrolean landscape.

    And of course, there had to be a little indulgence!

    We stopped for an aperitif at the Schlossbar Prösels, with a truly special view: the castle in front of us and vineyards all around.

    A toast with a beautiful view, the perfect end to a day full of nature, mountains, animals, good food and lots of lovely moments.

    Day 3 Tuesday 1 September · Alpe di SiusiThe Bullaccia and Rifugio Arnika

    For our third day, on the Alpe di Siusi (Seiser Alm), we had planned the Bullaccia (Puflatsch) circuit with our friends. The full loop, however, isn’t accessible with my wheelchair, so we found an alternative route to reach the Rifugio Arnika mountain hut anyway and all meet up for lunch.

    Going up to Compaccio

    We all drove together from Siusi to the valley station of the Alpe di Siusi cable car.

    Accessibility · Cable car

    The cable car is wheelchair accessible: getting into the cabin was simple and easy for me. For disabled people the ride up is free on showing your disability card.

    There is an accessible toilet both at the valley station in Siusi and at the top station in Compaccio.

    Up the Bullaccia with the Telemix

    Once in Compaccio (Compatsch), our friends set off on their walk, while we went to the Telemix Puflatsch, a lift that takes you straight up onto the Bullaccia.

    Accessibility · Telemix

    The Telemix is wheelchair accessible too. It is free for wheelchair users on showing the required documents. There is also an accessible toilet at the valley station.

    To reach the lift there’s a short unpaved stretch, which we managed with my Triride without any difficulty.

    Towards Rifugio Arnika

    Once at the top, we took the route towards the Rifugio Arnika, where we had arranged to meet our friends. With the meadows of the Alpe di Siusi and the Dolomites all around us, we enjoyed the landscape at our own pace.

    Among the meadows and the grazing cows

    The route turned out to be doable with my Triride, and the unpaved surface was easy going on the whole. Right along the way from the Bullaccia to the Rifugio Arnika we came across cows out grazing, which kept us company for a while: an encounter that made our walk even more special.

    Accessibility · A push needed

    On the short, steeper uphill stretches, the Triride’s wheel tended to slip. In those spots I needed a push from my companion to get over them. Otherwise, the route was pleasant and very manageable with my mobility aid.

    And so we reached the Rifugio Arnika, where we all met up for a well-earned lunch break. After the climb, sitting down together and sharing stories of the morning was even better.

    Back to Compaccio

    After lunch, our friends went on to complete the Bullaccia loop, while we headed back towards the Rifugio Bullaccia. From there we took the paved road that goes straight down to Compaccio.

    It was so satisfying: we managed the whole descent without having to take the Telemix again!

    Accessibility · Descent

    The way down is paved. Despite the slope, with my Triride’s cruise control we managed it without any problems, keeping a steady speed. This is our experience with my mobility aid: the slope should be judged according to the wheelchair you use and your ability to control the descent.

    Once in Compaccio, we met up with our friends again to take the cable car together back down to Siusi.

    Relaxing by the pool and dinner on the terrace

    Back at the hotel, after a day among the views of the Bullaccia, we treated ourselves to some well-deserved relaxation by the pool.

    Accessibility · Hotel pool

    The Hotel Emmy’s pool does not have a hoist for getting into the water. Even so, the sun-lounger area is a pleasant place to relax by the pool and enjoy the view.

    Our room was on the pool side: from our terrace, through a passage that is also wheelchair accessible, we could go straight to the sun-lounger area.

    Dinner on the terrace and sunset over Fiè

    And the day wasn’t over yet! We had booked a table for dinner on the terrace. The temperature was really pleasant, and eating outdoors while admiring the view was the right choice.

    To make it all even better came a beautiful sunset: the perfect end to a day of nature, mountains and so many small special moments.

    Day 4 Wednesday 2 September · Alpe di Siusi and OrtiseiBaita Sanon and Ortisei by night

    Our fourth day began with another day exploring the Alpe di Siusi: a walk among meadows and mountain chalets, lunch with friends at Baita Sanon and, to finish, an evening in lovely Ortisei.

    From Siusi to the alpine meadows

    We drove to Siusi and from there took the cable car up to Compaccio (Compatsch), in the heart of the Alpe di Siusi. Once at the top, we set off with our friends towards Baita Sanon.

    The first stretch is really pleasant: a wide road with an even surface crosses the meadows between traditional wooden chalets and spectacular Dolomite views. Walking all together, with the mountains around us, was a wonderful start to the day.

    Towards Baita Sanon

    We carried on together as far as a fork, where our routes split. We continued towards Baita Sanon; our friends chose the loop towards Monte Piz and the Adler Lodge Alpe, arranging to meet us all later at the chalet.

    Further on, the landscape keeps offering beautiful views of meadows, trees and Dolomite peaks. The surface, though, becomes more natural and uneven in places, with slopes that need more care.

    Accessibility · A push needed

    With my Triride All Road I managed the route, but on the hardest stretches I needed help from my companion. In my experience it’s doable with this mobility aid, as long as you’re not alone: the uneven surface and the slopes need care and, in places, a real helping hand.

    Once at Baita Sanon, we stopped to wait for our friends, enjoying the view and the peace of the alpine pasture.

    Lunch at Baita Sanon

    When we were all together again, it was finally time to sit down at the table. After the different routes of the morning, sharing lunch and telling each other about our walks was even more enjoyable.

    Accessibility · Toilet

    Baita Sanon has a toilet marked as accessible to wheelchair users.

    Heading back and a little relaxation

    After lunch we split up again: we went calmly back to Compaccio the same way we came, while our friends carried on towards Saltria.

    From Compaccio we took the cable car back down to Siusi and returned to the hotel. Before getting ready for the evening, we treated ourselves to some relaxation by the pool: a lovely break after the day on the Alpe.

    An after-dinner gem: Ortisei

    After dinner we had a walk in Ortisei, to see the village in its evening clothes. And I must say that, with the lights on, Ortisei really is a little gem!

    Accessibility · Parking

    We found the Conad supermarket car park very convenient, with several spaces reserved for disabled people: from there we reached the centre with my Triride.

    The illuminated Tyrolean houses, the shops and the shop windows create an elegant and evocative atmosphere. With the mountains as a backdrop, the village at night has a charm all of its own.

    Accessibility · Slopes

    The centre can be managed with my Triride, but the village is on a slope: there are ups and downs, especially in the streets of the old centre. I got around without any particular problems; on the steepest stretches help from a companion can be useful. Slopes should be judged according to the mobility aid you use.

    A walk that rounded off in the best way a day of Alpe di Siusi views, Baita Sanon, good food and a bit of relaxation. Another lovely memory to take with us, on my wheels.

    Day 5 Thursday 3 September · Ortisei and Val d’AnnaVal d’Anna

    On the fifth day we chose two different plans: our friends wanted to try a more demanding hike on the Alpe di Siusi, from Compatsch up to the Rifugio Bolzano (Schlernhaus), at 2,457 metres, also known as “the castle in the clouds” for its spectacular position on the Sciliar.

    We, on the other hand, took the chance to go back to my beloved Val d’Anna, a corner of Val Gardena I’m especially fond of.

    A green corner just outside Ortisei

    Val d’Anna is a quiet, green valley, with a stream running through it and woods all around. It’s a pleasant, relaxing place where nature really takes centre stage.

    Arriving and parking

    We headed for Ortisei. When we got to the Seceda area, the car park was already full. So we carried on towards Val d’Anna and, on reaching the paved road that leads straight into the valley, we discovered that access was restricted.

    Accessibility · Parking

    By showing our disabled parking badge, we were allowed through: so we were able to drive to the small Val d’Anna car park, close to the restaurant.

    Arriving by wheelchair from the Seceda

    Accessibility · Route

    If you want to start from the Seceda car park, the paved road to Val d’Anna can also be done in a wheelchair. Some uphill stretches can be hard work with a manual wheelchair, though, including for the companion who has to push. With an electric add-on like the Triride, the route is much easier to manage.

    Lunch and relaxing in the green

    And there’s another reason I love Val d’Anna so much: the food here is simply top marks! The Café Restaurant Val d’Anna is one of my favourite places, where you can enjoy excellent traditional South Tyrolean dishes in a beautiful green setting. For me, a stop here is almost compulsory!

    Once there, we settled onto two sun loungers under a parasol, with a little low table in front of us, perfect for having lunch comfortably without having to move.

    A great lunch and pure relaxation!

    A special sunset at the Hotel Emmy

    After a day of good food and relaxation in my beloved Val d’Anna, we went back to the Hotel Emmy.

    We ended the day with dinner at the hotel, accompanied by a spectacular sunset that made our evening even more special.

    Day 6 Friday 4 September · Sixth and last dayMonte Pana

    Sadly, the sixth and last day of our holiday in Fiè allo Sciliar arrived too. After breakfast we loaded the car and all set off together towards Monte Pana.

    After discovering Fiè, the Alpe di Siusi and Ortisei, it felt like a lovely way to say goodbye to these mountains. We were due to head home after lunch, so for our last morning we chose an easy, pleasant walk, with no rush, to spend a little more time all together.

    One last morning in the Dolomites

    Monte Pana is a beautiful green plateau. Meadows, woods and Dolomite views frame a walk that invites you to slow down and enjoy every moment.

    Arriving at Monte Pana

    There are two car parks on Monte Pana, P1 and P2, both handy for reaching the area where the trails start.

    Good to know before you go

    According to the information displayed at the time of our visit, parking is charged from 1 December to 31 March and from 1 June to 15 October, from 7 am to 2 pm; after 2 pm it is free.

    For our last day we chose the PanaRaida Adventure Trail: a route through meadows and woods at the foot of the Sassolungo (Langkofel), perfect for a peaceful morning.

    The PanaRaida Adventure Trail

    The PanaRaida is a loop of about 2.6 km, with a modest climb of around 50–60 metres. Along the way there are wooden stations and interactive installations: tree houses, games, mazes and swings make the walk fun and unusual.

    Accessibility · Route

    Accessibility of the trail

    The path is wide and compacted. With my Triride I managed the whole loop, but on the sloping stretches help from a companion was useful. Something to bear in mind if you’d like to do the walk on your own wheels.

    The biotope and a photo all together

    Along the loop there’s also a biotope with a Kneipp water-walking path, set in the beautiful natural surroundings of Monte Pana. The water, the greenery and the mountains in the background gave us another spot to remember.

    We also stopped for a few group photos: a lovely memory of this last walk, enjoyed together and with no rush.

    The last lunch

    Once we had finished the trail, we went back to the Monte Pana area for the last lunch of our holiday. We chose the Ristorante Alpinum, inside the historic Hotel Cendevaves.

    Lunch left us very satisfied and let us end our last day in the mountains at a gentle pace.

    Accessibility · Toilet

    Accessibility of the restaurant

    There is an accessible toilet inside the restaurant: a detail that always matters to me.

    Six days on my wheels

    After lunch it was time to say goodbye to these mountains and set off for home. Fiè allo Sciliar was a wonderful base for discovering lots of different places: the village and its lake, the Alpe di Siusi, Ortisei, Val d’Anna and finally Monte Pana.

    Six days flew by, leaving us with lots of happy memories, wonderful landscapes and new experiences to share. And above all the satisfaction of having been able to live these places on my wheels, without giving up the wish to discover and travel.

    And so our wonderful holiday comes to an end.

    My “no surprises” summary

    The walks, on my wheels

    The things I wish I had known before setting off, gathered here so they are easy to find: surfaces, slopes and those spots where a helping hand from your companion makes all the difference.

    How to read the stars

    The rating is about how easy the route is with my Triride and the help described in the story. The scale goes from 1 to 5: more stars, easier going. Five stars mean a route described as having no difficulty; four, generally easy going; three, stretches that need help; two, more marked difficulties; one, a very demanding route. They are not a rating of how beautiful a place is.

    The other routes mentioned

    The full Bullaccia loop was not accessible with my wheelchair. The Rifugio Bolzano, the loop towards Monte Piz and the Adler Lodge, and the continuation towards Saltria were routes chosen by our friends: I don’t give them stars because I didn’t do them on my wheels.

    From the terrace of our room, an accessible passage led to the sun-lounger area. The pool has no hoist. The Siusi–Compaccio cable car and the Telemix Puflatsch are wheelchair accessible.

    Overall experience

    5 hearts out of 5
