Miami and the Caribbean on my wheels

A few days in Miami and a Caribbean cruise aboard MSC World America.

  • 21 April to 3 May 2026
  • 4 nights in Miami · a week on a cruise
  • Florida · Honduras · Mexico · Bahamas

    This time my wheels crossed the ocean. Four nights in Miami awaited us, and then a week aboard MSC World America, calling at Roatán, Costa Maya, Cozumel and Ocean Cay, in the Bahamas.

    Day 1 Tuesday 21 AprilFrom Rome to Miami

    Our journey began at Rome Fiumicino airport, with the direct flight to Miami. For anyone travelling in a wheelchair, the departure deserves to be told too: the assistance, the transfers and the transport of mobility aids are all part of the experience.

    Assistance on the plane and the Triride

    For me we chose WCHC assistance, meant for passengers who cannot walk and need full assistance, including help with transfers all the way to their seat on the plane.

    Accessibility · In-flight assistance

    The assistance continues during the flight and includes help reaching the toilet, using the on-board wheelchair. An important service on a long journey like the one from Rome to Miami. Being accompanied to the toilet does not, however, include lifting the passenger or personal assistance inside it: for those you need a companion.

    I also took my Triride with me, to use it with my wheelchair during the trip.

    Accessibility · Triride and battery

    At check-in we were asked to detach the battery from the Triride: the battery travelled in the cabin, while the Triride went in the hold.

    My advice is to label everything you take on the plane with your name, address and phone number, especially mobility aids and any parts that can be separated: wheelchair, Triride, battery and the transfer board too, if you have one. A simple precaution, but a useful one at boarding and when your things are handed back.

    Arrival and picking up the car

    Once we had landed in Miami, we went to the car hire point near the airport and picked up the car. From there we set off for our hotel, the Pestana Miami South Beach.

    Accessibility · Hire car

    Hiring a car left me free to plan the excursions, choosing where to go and when to stop. With the transfer board, which I use to move from the wheelchair to the car seat, I can travel by car and reach the places I want to visit.

    During our stay we found getting around Miami by car easy and straightforward.

    Good to know before you go

    The car parks are convenient, but really very expensive: a cost to budget for when planning the trip.

    I did not use my Italian disabled parking permit because I didn’t know the local rules well and didn’t want to risk fines that could have complicated the trip.

    I found out later that in Florida a valid foreign permit, showing the international wheelchair symbol, is recognised for using disabled parking spaces. Whether parking is free, however, depends on the rules of each car park. Something I wish I had known before leaving.

    Our hotel: Pestana Miami South Beach

    When we arrived at the Pestana, we dropped off our luggage and settled into our room.

    Accessibility · Hotel

    I checked the hotel’s accessible features in person: a platform lift to reach the floor, and a bathroom in the room with an adapted WC and a level-access shower.

    There is also a beautiful outdoor pool with a hoist chair for getting into the water. These details matter to me, because accessibility also means being able to enjoy the spaces and the relaxing moments of a holiday.

    Our first evening on Española Way

    With the luggage sorted, we went out for a walk, leaving the car at the hotel. The Pestana is central, so we could reach the heart of South Beach on foot and with my wheelchair.

    For dinner I had planned to go to Española Way, a little pedestrian street with a Mediterranean feel. The warm-coloured buildings, the palm trees and the outdoor tables are reminiscent of a small Spanish village, in the heart of Miami Beach.

    In the evening it is especially charming: strings of lights illuminate the street, the bars and restaurants come alive and people stroll between them. We found it very lively and bright, a lovely setting for our first dinner in Miami.

    After dinner we went back to the hotel. Between the flight, picking up the car and our first walk, it had been a long day: time to rest, before starting to discover Miami.

    Day 2 Wednesday 22 AprilThe ocean, the colours of Wynwood and an evening in Little Havana

    Breakfast at Rosetta Bakery

    Our first full day in Miami started with breakfast at Rosetta Bakery, an Italian-style bakery where we found a little corner of home even on the other side of the ocean. We had two excellent cappuccinos and two giant filled croissants: a pleasant, delicious stop before setting off to explore South Beach.

    The walk along the ocean

    We headed for the Beach Boardwalk, the seafront promenade, also known as the Beachwalk. From here we enjoyed wonderful views of the ocean, between the beach and the palm trees.

    Standing out on the sand are the iconic lifeguard towers, with their colours and unusual shapes: one of the most recognisable images of Miami Beach. Seeing them in real life, with the ocean behind them, made us feel we were truly on our trip.

    Ocean Drive and the Versace mansion

    We carried on towards Ocean Drive, one of the landmarks of South Beach. The Art Deco hotels, the colourful façades and the bars and restaurants facing the street make up the Miami we have seen so many times in photographs and films.

    Along the way we also came across Gianni Versace’s former home, Casa Casuarina, with its unmistakable entrance. A stop that caught the eye during our walk along this famous street.

    South Pointe Park and the pier

    We then reached South Pointe Park, at the southern tip of Miami Beach. Here the green spaces open onto the sea and the path led us towards South Pointe Pier, the pier stretching out over the ocean.

    It is a lovely spot for admiring the coast and the long beach of South Beach, from a different angle to the one we had in the morning.

    For lunch we stopped for a quick bite, giving ourselves a short break before carrying on with the day.

    The afternoon at Wynwood Walls

    We went back to the hotel and took the car out of the car park to visit Wynwood Walls. We found a convenient car park nearby.

    From the ocean we moved to a completely different Miami, made of urban art and huge painted walls. Wynwood Walls is an open-air street art museum, created in 2009, where international artists have turned the walls into works to look at one after another.

    Among figures, geometric patterns and intense colours, each mural speaks its own language. It is a visit that invites you to linger over the details, and it added another side to our discovery of Miami.

    The evening in Little Havana

    For the evening we moved on to Little Havana, the heart of Cuban culture in Miami. We found a paid car park nearby, fairly expensive but convenient, and started our walk.

    Along Calle Ocho, the neighbourhood’s best-known street, there is one cigar shop, little store, café and bar after another. The signs, the colours and the music tell of the bond with Cuba and give the walk a different atmosphere from South Beach.

    Among the curiosities of the walk were the roosters and hens we met along the street. Their presence goes back to the tradition of keeping poultry in family backyards, including families of Cuban and Latin American origin. Over time some of the birds started living and breeding freely in the neighbourhood, becoming a characteristic sight in Little Havana. We saw them walking near the pavements and the parked cars, like little residents of the district. We stopped to watch them and to photograph a rooster with red, golden and dark feathers: an unusual and charming detail that added character to our visit to Little Havana.

    We also passed the characteristic players’ meeting place, Máximo Gómez Park, known as Domino Park: a neighbourhood gathering spot, famous for its games of dominoes and the chatter around the tables.

    Dinner and music at Old’s Havana

    For dinner we chose Old’s Havana Cuban Bar & Cocina, on Calle Ocho. The retro-style décor and the music accompanied us through an evening with a Cuban flavour.

    We started with a mojito, then enjoyed the traditional dinner and the music. After the ocean in the morning and the murals in the afternoon, Little Havana gave us yet another picture of Miami, made of flavours and rhythms.

    Day 3 Thursday 23 AprilThe Everglades, Bayside and Miami by night

    On the third day we left Miami in our hire car to reach Shark Valley, in Everglades National Park. The landscape changes completely: the buildings and streets of the city give way to a vast expanse of water and vegetation.

    We left the car in the car park and joined a guided tour aboard a sightseeing tram, covered by a roof and open at the sides, which lets you watch the landscape and the animals along the route.

    Accessibility · Tram

    Accessibility of the tram — checked in person

    The staff remove a few seats to make room for the wheelchair. You board by a ramp, and the wheelchair is hooked in and safely secured. I was able to take part in the tour staying in my own wheelchair, without having to transfer to another seat.

    I preferred this option to the typical airboat rides, the boats with the big propeller at the back. I was looking for a fuller visit, with the guide’s explanations and the chance to reach the viewpoint.

    The guided tour through nature

    The tram follows a loop of about 24 kilometres (15 miles), through marshy prairies and canals. Along the way, the guide tells the story of this unusual ecosystem and helps you recognise the animals that live in it, starting with the alligators.

    Accessibility · Toilet

    Accessible toilets at the starting point

    At the Shark Valley Visitor Center, in the area where the tram leaves from, there are accessible toilets.

    The observation tower

    Halfway round we stopped at the Shark Valley Observation Tower, the viewing tower from which you can take in the vastness of the Everglades.

    From above, water and vegetation seem to spread out endlessly. It is a perspective that helps you understand this landscape, often described as a great “river of grass”.

    Accessibility · Ramp

    Accessibility of the tower

    The viewpoint can be reached in a wheelchair by a ramp: I was able to enjoy this part of the visit too. The climb has a gradient to bear in mind and, depending on your needs, you may need help from a companion.

    The visit continues on the footpaths

    Once off the tram, you can keep exploring the area along the footpaths, taking in the landscape at a slower pace.

    Accessibility · Paths

    Accessible paths

    The main road of the loop is paved and flat. Near the visitor centre there is also the Bobcat Boardwalk, an accessible boardwalk that lets you carry on discovering the Everglades. Not all the trails in the area have the same features, though.

    Admission and the guided tour

    Admission to the park, including access to the car park, costs 35 dollars per car, plus 100 dollars per person aged 16 and over for visitors who are not resident in the United States. The surcharge does not apply to anyone entering with a recognised park pass that covers it, such as the America the Beautiful pass.

    The guided tram tour is paid for separately: 36 dollars per adult, or 27 dollars for those aged 62 and over.

    The prices are fairly high, but for me it is worth it.

    A stop at Bayside Marketplace

    We got back in the car and, on the way back, stopped for an early evening snack-cum-dinner at Bayside Marketplace, the open-air shopping centre overlooking Biscayne Bay, in the heart of Downtown Miami.

    Shops and restaurants cluster around the marina, among moored boats, palm trees and glimpses of the water. Beside the bay rises the big Skyviews Miami observation wheel.

    Accessibility · Toilet

    Accessible toilet at Bayside Marketplace

    During our stop I used one of the shopping centre’s accessible toilets.

    An evening stroll on Ocean Drive

    After calling in at the hotel, we went back to Ocean Drive for an evening stroll, among the illuminated hotel façades and the lively bars.

    Towards the southern end of the street, in the direction of South Pointe Pier, we stopped at an Italian ice-cream parlour for a giant ice cream.

    Then we took a little wander around the bridge area, among glimpses of the water and tall illuminated skyscrapers. The lights of the buildings traced the city’s skyline in the evening.

    Day 4 Friday 24 AprilThe Florida Keys and sunset in Key West

    We devoted the whole of the fourth day to the Florida Keys, driving to Key West in our hire car and making several stops along the way.

    Leaving Miami, we took the Overseas Highway, the road that links the islands by a succession of bridges, with the sea opening up on either side. The landscape changes slowly: the city gives way to tropical vegetation, small marinas and the countless shades of the water.

    Crossing Key Largo and carrying on towards Islamorada, we sank deeper and deeper into the atmosphere of the Keys, among palm trees, colourful little houses and bars overlooking the sea. Here even the drive becomes a wonderful part of the day.

    Feeding the tarpon at Robbie’s

    In Islamorada we stopped at Robbie’s, where you can feed the tarpon, big fish that gather in the water beside the pier.

    Naturally we wanted to try it too! You hold the bait fish out over the water and the tarpon come close, ready to snatch it with a sudden lunge. Seeing them so near is truly impressive: an unusual, fun experience that made this stop one of the most memorable moments of the day.

    The setting has its own charm too, with the wooden piers, the boats and that laid-back, tropical air that accompanies the whole journey through the Keys.

    The old bridge and the Seven Mile Bridge

    Back on the road, we carried on towards Marathon, stopping in the area of the old Seven Mile Bridge.

    Here you can see both the historic bridge and the modern one, crossing a wide stretch of sea to link the islands. The old bridge preserves the memory of the railway that once ran all the way to Key West; beside it, the new Seven Mile Bridge now carries the traffic of the Overseas Highway.

    It is one of the most striking views of the route: a long line suspended over the water, with the sea all around and a horizon that seems never to end.

    Arriving in Key West and parking

    Carrying on through the Lower Keys, we finally reached Key West, our furthest destination of the day.

    We left the car at the Old Town Garage, the city car park, and from there continued towards the centre.

    Accessibility · Toilet

    In the car park I used the toilet, which I found fairly accessible.

    Key West’s unusual cemetery

    Leaving the car park, on the way to the centre we passed Key West’s unusual cemetery.

    With its tropical vegetation, small mausoleums and many above-ground tombs, this place looks different from the cemeteries we are used to.

    It holds a curiosity too: some gravestones carry unusual and even humorous inscriptions, such as “I told you I was sick” or “I’m just resting my eyes”. Little phrases that tell of the character of the people remembered and the nonconformist spirit of the island.

    A late lunch at the Old Town Tavern

    We stopped at the Old Town Tavern & Beer Garden, on Duval Street, when it was already quite late: more of an afternoon snack than a lunch!

    The place was one of those recommended to us when we were looking for somewhere characterful to eat in Key West. An informal, unusual setting, with wooden features and an inner courtyard full of greenery.

    We ate among palm trees and hanging ferns, surrounded by white balconies, well-worn tables and colourful chairs, with music accompanying this pleasant break. A little tropical corner to stop in and soak up the atmosphere of the island.

    Hemingway’s house, unfortunately already closed

    After eating we went to Ernest Hemingway’s house, now a museum.

    Good to know before you go

    We would have liked to visit it, but unfortunately we found it already closed: the house museum closes at 5 pm. With the drive and the various stops along the Keys, we got there too late. A small disappointment, and a useful thing to remember when planning the day.

    The Southernmost Point and a stroll in the centre

    Among the stops on our visit was the Southernmost Point, one of Key West’s most famous symbols: the big, colourful buoy-shaped marker with the words “90 Miles to Cuba”, a reminder of how close Cuba is, just 90 miles away.

    We then strolled through the lively centre, among light-coloured wooden houses, verandas, shops, bars and venues full of music.

    Key West has an atmosphere all of its own: lively, informal and relaxed, with an American character blended with a Caribbean air. We enjoyed the walk, looking at the buildings and the little details along the streets, before reaching the square by the sea.

    The famous sunset at Mallory Square

    Finally we headed to Mallory Square, where people gather to admire Key West’s famous sunset.

    We waited for the sun to sink towards the horizon, while the sky and the water took on ever warmer colours. After the stops and discoveries of the day, we paused to enjoy this spectacle: a beautiful moment with which to say goodbye to the island.

    The drive back to Miami

    After the sunset we went back to the Old Town Garage to collect the car.

    Accessibility · Toilet

    Before setting off we went back to the car park toilet, which I had found fairly accessible, and I used it again.

    Then we took the road back to Miami, crossing the islands and the bridges of the Overseas Highway once more.

    We got back very late, tired but happy, with the views of the Keys and the colours of the Key West sunset still in our eyes.

    Day 5 Saturday 25 AprilFrom Miami to MSC World America

    On the fifth day we said goodbye to Miami to begin a new part of our holiday: the cruise on MSC World America.

    Good to know before you go

    We returned the hire car in the city centre and then took the taxi we had booked to reach the Port of Miami, planning to arrive with plenty of time to spare.

    That turned out to be a really good decision: the port was extremely busy! Between taxis, cars, shuttles and passengers with suitcases, the movement never stopped.

    That day, in fact, besides our MSC World America, which can carry up to 6,764 passengers, Royal Caribbean’s Icon of the Seas was also due to sail: famous for having entered service as the largest cruise ship in the world, with a maximum capacity of 7,600 passengers.

    Two real floating cities: thinking of their size, it is easy to see how heavy the traffic around the port could be! Having left in good time let us face the transfer much more calmly.

    Stepping on board: we were left open-mouthed

    Once inside MSC World America, we were left open-mouthed.

    Before us opened the World Galleria, a large indoor space on several levels, with bars, shops and the upper decks looking down onto it: it felt like walking into an elegant palace on the sea.

    Looking up, we were enchanted by the enormous LED ceiling, showing a succession of images of the sea, with whales and turtles swimming above us. It felt like being underwater, gazing up at a moving ocean.

    The images, the colours and the play of light made the entrance even more spectacular. It was only our first encounter with the ship, but we were already full of wonder: we couldn’t wait to explore it!

    A first look around and the view over Miami

    While waiting to be let into our cabin, we used the time for a first look around.

    Out on the open decks, we found ourselves facing a beautiful view over Miami: the skyscrapers, the water of the bay and the port, seen from above, gave us a new perspective on the city we had explored over the previous days.

    We stopped to enjoy the view, as we started getting to know what would be our home for the cruise.

    The cabin and its enormous balcony

    When we walked into the cabin, it was another “wow!”.

    The cabin was accessible, of course, and what also won us over was the enormous balcony: a beautiful space from which to look out over the sea and enjoy the voyage.

    Good to know before you go

    The shower seat was not very comfortable and had no armrests. Depending on your mobility and support needs, it may be worth asking the staff for a plastic chair with armrests suitable for use in the shower, checking that one is available and that it is stable. It is a detail to weigh up according to your own situation.

    Exploring the ship

    After the compulsory safety drill, we went back to wandering around the ship, very curious to discover it.

    Between the indoor spaces and the open decks, every corner invited us to keep going. The size was astonishing and we had only just begun to find our bearings: there was still so much to see!

    Then it was time for dinner, which was pleasant and let us relax before carrying on with our first evening on board.

    An evening of music and lights

    After dinner we went out onto the World Promenade, the outdoor promenade that for us was the most beautiful part of the ship.

    Here we watched a sound and light show: the big palm-shaped structures lit up and changed colour in time with the music, turning the whole space into a glowing stage set.

    With the sea all around and the show in front of us, we enjoyed this last moment of wonder of the day. After Miami and the Keys, our Caribbean adventure had only just begun!

    Day 6 Sunday 26 AprilA day at sea, relaxing on board

    The sixth day was spent entirely at sea, a chance to enjoy MSC World America at our leisure, alternating relaxation with exploring the ship.

    For this cruise we had chosen the Aurea experience, which came with several benefits: access to the reserved sun deck on deck 20, access to the spa at no extra cost, and the option of dining at any time, without being tied to a fixed sitting. A convenience that let us organise our days at our own pace.

    The morning on the sun deck

    In the morning we went to our reserved sun deck, on the twentieth deck, to treat ourselves to a bit of relaxation.

    With the sea all around and the ship under way, we enjoyed this first day with no excursions or transfers: just the pleasure of being on board and taking our time.

    Lunch at the buffet and exploring the ship

    For lunch we went to the buffet, then carried on wandering around to discover the ship.

    After the wonder of embarkation day, we were starting to find our way around better, but there were still so many spaces to get to know. We made the most of the day at sea to explore them at leisure, letting curiosity guide us.

    The attractions on the top decks

    On our wanderings we also went up to the attractions area, on the ship’s top decks. Here is The Harbour, an open-air space that feels like a small amusement park at sea, with water slides, games and a ropes course suspended among ropes and walkways.

    Most striking of all is the Cliffhanger, the big swing over the sea: its arms carry the seats out beyond the edge of the ship, swinging riders about 50 metres above the water. Just watching it is quite something!

    This area is also home to the entrance of the big spiral slide, which drops a full eleven decks down to the World Promenade. Between these attractions and the view over the ocean, this part of the ship left us open-mouthed too.

    Dinner and the evening show

    In the evening we went to dinner, making the most of the flexible dining time included in our Aurea experience.

    After dinner we watched the evening show, rounding off a day of relaxation, sea and new discoveries on board.

    Day 7 Monday 27 AprilRoatán: views, sloths and the Caribbean Sea

    On the seventh day we went ashore at Roatán, an island of Honduras in the Caribbean Sea.

    Through GetYourGuide we had booked an excursion with Roatan Trips and Tours (RT&T), choosing a private car with a driver so we could visit the island at our own pace. The excursion included pick-up from and return to the cruise terminal, a scenic stop, a visit to an animal park and a beach break with a sun lounger included.

    A scenic stop on the island

    Before reaching the animal park, our driver took us to a viewpoint, from which we could see MSC World America docked in the port.

    It was lovely to see our ship from a distance, set in the island’s landscape: a different perspective from the one we had got used to on board.

    The visit to the sloth park

    The next stop was Manawakie Park, where we saw parrots, sloths and monkeys.

    Between the colours of the parrots, the slow movements of the sloths and the liveliness of the monkeys, it was an unusual and enjoyable visit. The most moving moment was being able to cuddle the sloths: having them so close, with their calm expression, is a special memory of the day.

    Good to know before you go

    The park is accessible, but to reach the animal area you have to get up a very steep slope. The staff helped us with this stretch, so we could carry on with the visit.

    The beach break

    After visiting the park, we moved on to the beach at Foster’s West Bay Resort.

    At the entrance there was an attendant checking who came in, and he let us through because we had already booked. We reached a lovely beach bar overlooking the sea, right in front of the beach and the sun loungers. Use of the sun lounger was included in the beach break that came with the excursion.

    Here we treated ourselves to a pleasant break with a snack and a cocktail, enjoying the sea of Roatán, with its pale sand, turquoise water and tropical vegetation.

    Accessibility · Beach

    The beach was accessible.

    Back to the cruise terminal

    At the end of the break, our driver took us back to the cruise terminal, as planned in the excursion, to go back on board MSC World America.

    Day 8 Tuesday 28 AprilCosta Maya: Chacchoben and Mahahual

    On the eighth day we went ashore at Costa Maya, in Mexico, for a day between the history of the Maya civilisation and the Caribbean Sea.

    We had booked a private excursion with a car and driver, which included a guided visit to the archaeological site of Chacchoben and a stop on the beach at Mahahual, with a sun lounger included.

    The first stop was Chacchoben, set deep in tropical vegetation. Ancient pyramids and stone buildings emerge among the trees: an atmospheric place, which we discovered thanks to our guide’s explanations.

    The visit was very interesting, even though it was in English rather than Italian. The explanations helped us understand the history of the site and of the Maya civilisation better.

    Good to know before you go

    I used the toilet at the site: it was basic and not adapted for disabled people, but the wheelchair could get in.

    After the visit, our driver took us to Mahahual, where we had our beach break at the Maramao Beach Club, overlooking the Caribbean Sea.

    Here we had a light lunch with a cocktail, and then treated ourselves to some relaxation by the sea, with the sun lounger included in the excursion. Our parasols were the palm trees: we settled in their shade, enjoying this Caribbean break.

    Accessibility · Beach

    The staff helped me reach the sun lounger on the beach.

    After a pleasant day of history and sea, we went back to the ship.

    Day 9 Wednesday 29 AprilCozumel: cacao, tequila and turquoise sea

    On the ninth day of our holiday MSC World America took us to Cozumel, where we joined the “Easy and accessible: Cozumel” excursion, bought directly from MSC, with a scenic drive through the centre and two stops devoted to Mexican flavours and traditions.

    The first stop was the Mayan Cacao Company, to learn about cacao and its link with the Maya civilisation. For the Maya it was a precious food, important in social and religious life; its beans were even used as currency.

    During the visit we tasted “the gold of the Maya”, cacao, and tried the Chocolate Margarita, a local speciality that combines tequila and chocolate: an unusual meeting of two flavours that symbolise Mexico.

    The excursion continued to Barriecito, where we listened to a very interesting talk about tequila. They told us about its link with the agave plant, the stages of production and the differences between the various types. A chance to get to know this drink, and the tradition behind it, a little better.

    Before going back on board we also took an orientation tour around the port area and along the seafront of San Miguel de Cozumel, stopping to take a few photographs.

    Among palm trees, shops and buildings facing the sea, we enjoyed the views of the town. Near the shore the water was so clear you could see the rocky seabed, while further out it turned turquoise and deep blue.

    From the seafront we also photographed our MSC World America, which looked even more imposing from land, framed by the turquoise sea and a sky crossed by big white clouds.

    Accessibility · Ship’s pool

    We went back on board a little early, when the ship was not yet crowded, and took the chance to enjoy its large accessible pool. Wonderful! A relaxing moment that rounded off our day in Cozumel in the best possible way.

    Day 10 Thursday 30 AprilA day at sea, relaxation and the White Party

    The tenth day was spent at sea, a chance to enjoy MSC World America and have a relaxing day.

    Accessibility · Spa

    After the lovely experiences of the excursions, always a pleasure for us, we discovered another side of life on board, treating ourselves to some time in the ship’s spa, which is wheelchair accessible too. A pleasant break to look after ourselves and be pampered.

    In the evening, though, it was party time with the White Party! White was the star of the evening, among music, fun and a lively party atmosphere.

    A day with two different rhythms: first the calm of the spa, then the fun of the evening. That, too, is the beauty of a cruise!

    Day 11 Friday 1 MayOcean Cay, one last swim in the Bahamas

    The eleventh day gave us one last day by the sea at Ocean Cay MSC Marine Reserve, the island in the Bahamas reserved for MSC guests. White sandy beaches, turquoise water and a distinctive lighthouse: a beautiful setting in which to end our cruise.

    We hired a parasol and settled on the beach to enjoy the sea and a bit of relaxation.

    Accessibility · Beach

    For me the best moment was being able to swim, thanks to the accessibility of the beach. We used a beach wheelchair, designed for moving over sand, which let me reach the sea and enjoy the water of the Bahamas.

    These are the things that make the difference: being able to truly experience a beach, getting into the water and taking part in the day.

    For lunch we ate under our parasol, using the MSC catering that is available on the island too. A pleasant break, with the sea in front of us and still a little time to savour the day.

    Then we took a walk around the island as far as the lighthouse, one of the symbols of Ocean Cay, admiring other glimpses of beach and the different shades of the sea.

    In the end we went back on board reluctantly: it was the last day of the cruise and we would have liked to stay a little longer.

    After so many places visited and so many different experiences, we were also taking home the memory of that swim in the Bahamas. A beautiful way to say goodbye to the trip.

    Day 12 Saturday 2 MayBack to Miami and goodbye to our holiday

    On the twelfth day MSC World America arrived in Miami: it was time to say goodbye to the ship and end our cruise.

    We had breakfast on board and disembarked at our leisure. We had no scheduled disembarkation time, so we could get off without hurrying, enjoying our last moments on the ship.

    For the return we had already arranged the transfer from the port to the airport, where the journey home awaited us.

    The journey home ended in Italy on Sunday 3 May 2026.

    And so our fantastic holiday came to an end, between the days in Miami, the excursions, the Caribbean Sea and the moments we shared on board.

    I came home happy and satisfied to have been able to take such an important trip, in spite of everything. A trip I had longed for and was able to truly experience, on my wheels: visiting new places, swimming in the sea and bringing home so many wonderful memories.

    That, too, is what I want to share with “Travelling on my wheels”: the joy of setting off and discovering, together with the accessibility information that makes these experiences possible.

    My “no surprises” summary

    The things to remember, gathered here so they are easy to find.

    Things to remember

    The flight and mobility aids

    • WCHC assistance: on our trip we requested full assistance all the way to the seat on the plane. On board, the aisle wheelchair means you can be accompanied to the toilet; lifting and personal assistance inside it require a companion.
    • Triride and battery: the Triride’s removable battery has to be detached and carried in the cabin, because it cannot travel in the hold. The Triride, without its battery, goes in the hold.
    • Labels: mark the wheelchair, Triride, battery and transfer board with your name and contact details.

    Miami and the Florida Keys

    • Hire car: I use the transfer board to move between wheelchair and car seat. The car let us plan the excursions at our own pace.
    • Car parks: convenient but expensive, in Little Havana too. A foreign permit being recognised for disabled parking spaces does not automatically mean parking is free.
    • Pestana Miami South Beach: checked in person: platform lift, adapted WC, level-access shower and pool with a hoist chair.
    • Shark Valley: tram with a ramp and the wheelchair secured on board. Mention the wheelchair when you book. The tower is reached by a ramp, with a gradient that may call for help. Not all the trails have the same accessibility features.
    • Tickets at Shark Valley: park admission and the tram are paid for separately; admission is paid by card.
    • Key West: the day trip from Miami is a long one. Hemingway’s house was already closed when we arrived: in our story, closing time is 5 pm.

    The ship and the excursions

    • Port of Miami: leave plenty of time for the transfer; on our embarkation day the traffic was very heavy.
    • Accessible cabin: the shower seat had no armrests. Think about the support you need and ask the staff whether a suitable chair is available, checking that it is stable.
    • Large pool and spa: wheelchair accessible too.
    • Benefits of the Aurea experience: access to the reserved sun deck on deck 20, access to the spa at no extra cost, and dinner at any time, with no fixed sitting.
    • Roatán: private excursion with a driver, with pick-up from and return to the terminal. To reach the animals there was a very steep slope, managed with help from the staff. The beach at Foster’s West Bay Resort was accessible and the sun lounger was included in our excursion.
    • Chacchoben: guide in English; the toilet I used was not adapted, although the wheelchair could get in.
    • Mahahual: at the Maramao Beach Club the staff helped me reach the sun lounger on the beach; the sun lounger was included in the excursion.
    • Cozumel: we joined the “Easy and accessible: Cozumel” excursion, bought directly from MSC.
    • Ocean Cay: we reached the sea with a beach wheelchair made for sand; the parasol was hired.
    • Return: transfer from the port to the airport arranged in advance.
